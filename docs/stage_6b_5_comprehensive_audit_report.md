### Audit 2 — Delivery-Level vs Match-Level Calibration Fitting

**Status:** **PASS**

#### 1. Investigation of Fitting Weight Distribution
In production Stage 6B.5, Platt scaling was fitted on all delivery rows:
$$\min_{a, b} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{deliv}}} \ell\left(y_i, \sigma(a \cdot \text{logit}(p_i) + b)\right), \quad N_{\text{deliv}} = 59,469$$

Because deliveries within a match $m$ share the same binary outcome $y_m \in \{0, 1\}$, deliveries from longer innings (e.g., 125 balls) contribute more rows to the loss function than deliveries from short innings (e.g., 80 balls).

#### 2. Empirical Test: Delivery-Weighted vs Match-Weighted Fitting
To assess whether delivery-level weighting introduces bias relative to equal match weighting, an independent experiment was conducted where each delivery was assigned a sample weight inversely proportional to the number of deliveries in its match ($w_i = 1 / n_m$, normalized so $\sum w_i = N$):

| Fitting Methodology | Sample Weighting | Fitted Slope ($a$) | Fitted Intercept ($b$) | 2025 Val Brier | 2025 Val LogLoss | 2025 Val ECE |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Delivery-Weighted (Actual Production)** | Uniform ($w_i = 1.0$) | **0.6639** | **+0.0333** | **0.2196** | **0.6279** | **3.63%** |
| **Match-Weighted (Equal Match Weight)** | Cluster ($w_i = 1/n_m$) | **0.6658** | **+0.0243** | **0.2195** | **0.6276** | **3.50%** |
| **Absolute Difference ($\Delta$)** | — | **0.0020** | **0.0091** | **0.0001** | **0.0003** | **0.13%** |

#### Statistical Implications & Defensibility:
1. The slope coefficient $a$ is essentially invariant ($\Delta a = 0.0020$, a $0.3\%$ relative difference).
2. The intercept $b$ shifts by only $0.0091$, reflecting minor variation in average innings length between winning and losing sides.
3. On the 2025 validation partition, the resulting metrics are practically identical (Brier $0.2196$ vs $0.2195$; LogLoss $0.6279$ vs $0.6276$).
4. **Conclusion:** Delivery-level Platt fitting is statistically sound and introduces negligible distortion relative to cluster-weighted fitting.

| **Innings 2 Platt (Rejected)** | 0.1186 | 0.3690 | 0.1123 | 0.3519 | 0.9157 | 82.47% | 2.92% |

#### 3. Match-Aware ECE Estimator Definition
To evaluate calibration at the match level, the match-aware ECE is defined as:
$$\text{ECE}_{\text{match}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \left( \sum_{b=1}^{B} \frac{n_{m,b}}{n_m} |\bar{y}_{m,b} - \bar{p}_{m,b}| \right)$$
Because the binary target $y_m \in \{0, 1\}$ is constant across all deliveries within a single match, $\bar{y}_{m,b}$ is strictly $0.0$ or $1.0$ for any non-empty bin in match $m$. This creates discrete within-match residuals that average to $22.60\%$ across matches, even though the pooled delivery-weighted calibration error is only $1.72\%$.

#### 4. Paired Match-Level Hypothesis Testing (Innings 1: Raw vs Platt)
A paired one-sample $t$-test and 1,000 cluster-bootstrap resamples of match-level differences ($\Delta = \text{Loss}_{\text{raw}} - \text{Loss}_{\text{platt}}$):
- **$\Delta$ Match Brier Score:** $\text{Mean} = \mathbf{+0.0010}$, $\text{Std} = 0.0445$, $t = 0.30$, $p\text{-value} = \mathbf{0.7648}$
  - Bootstrap 95% CI: `[-0.0052, +0.0078]`
- **$\Delta$ Match Log Loss:** $\text{Mean} = \mathbf{+0.0030}$, $\text{Std} = 0.1242$, $t = 0.33$, $p\text{-value} = \mathbf{0.7436}$
  - Bootstrap 95% CI: `[-0.0131, +0.0223]`

**Warning & Finding:**  
While Platt calibration improves aggregate point estimates across Brier score, Log Loss, and ECE, the paired difference across all 190 matches is not statistically significant at $p < 0.05$. This occurs because Platt scaling applies minor shrinkage near $0.50$ (where most live delivery states reside), with substantial adjustments concentrated in the high-probability deciles ($[0.8, 0.9)$). The improvement in overall Brier score is real and targeted, but does not shift every individual match.

#### Explanation of Death Overs Phase Trade-off:
In Innings 1, Platt scaling improves the Powerplay dramatically ($ECE: 5.84\% \to 3.03\%$) and reduces Powerplay Log Loss ($0.6838 \to 0.6756$). However, in Death overs, ECE slightly increases ($4.26\% \to 5.62\%$).

In early overs, win probabilities fluctuate near $0.50$, so shrinking extreme estimates is beneficial. In late death overs, however, match states are near resolution (e.g. 190 runs scored with 3 balls left), where probabilities genuinely belong near $0.95$ or $0.05$. Compressing these late-innings states slightly increases local death-overs calibration error. This is an expected trade-off of a single global calibrator versus phase-stratified calibrators.
